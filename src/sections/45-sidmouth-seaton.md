The steep one: up Salcombe Hill out of Sidmouth, over Dunscombe and down to
Weston Mouth, then Branscombe Mouth, the Hooken landslip and Beer Head into Beer
and along to Seaton — 753 m of climbing in 15.6 km.

There is nothing between Sidmouth and Branscombe Mouth, nearly 10 km of cliff
and combe with no road down but the lanes at Weston and Salcombe Regis. The Sea
Shanty on the beach at Branscombe is the only food before Beer.

Getting back is easy in a way section 44 is not: the 9A runs hourly seven days
a week, Seaton to Sidmouth in about half an hour, last bus 20:35 Mon–Sat and
19:30 on a Sunday. It goes inland over the top, though — for Beer and Branscombe
the only bus is the 899, and there are five journeys each way Mon–Fri, three on
Saturdays and **nothing at all on a Sunday**. So on a Sunday those ten roadless
kilometres have no exit but on foot, or by taxi: Karen's Cars is based in
Branscombe itself, takes dogs and carries walkers' luggage, which makes it the
only vehicle in the middle of this section. The 899 is not a summer bus — it
runs to the same times in January — but its operator's own timetable online is
two years old, so ring before relying on a printed time.

Neither Sidmouth's nor Seaton's injury service opens at a weekend, and neither
has an X-ray. Saturday or Sunday, or anything that might be broken, means
Honiton (8am–8pm every day, 13 km) or the A&E at Exeter. The Seaton Tramway
starts 470 m from the finish and runs up the Axe to Colyford and Colyton — an
afternoon out rather than a way home.
